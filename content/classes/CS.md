I've taken several CS department courses during my time at Berkeley. Here are my thoughts on them, in the order I took them.

## CS 61A (Fall 24)
I took 61A in my first semester at Berkeley.

It was a great course! I had DeNero, and he was as great as his reputation suggests. Prior to taking 61A, I did have coding experience from high school. This really helped me suceed in the course and made it pretty chill. I was able to focus more on the conceptual side of things (trees, linked lists, etc) rather than being caught up in syntax and fluency issues.

Overall, 61A was a well-designed course for someone with prior coding experience.

## CS 61B (Spring 25)
I took 61B in my second semester at Berkeley.

61B might be the most useful CS course I've taken. This kind of goes without saying -- data structures & algorithms are ubiquitous concepts in CS.

I regret that in Spring 2025, the packed courseload I took came at the cost of long-term retention. This particularly impacted 61B -- I did well on the exams, but if you stopped me on the street and asked me what a red-black tree is, I could not tell you.

Often, I find myself learning a lot while taking an exam (as opposed to learning *before* the exam). I particularly experienced this with 61B -- I found myself learning a lot from my cheatsheet during each exam. I probably spent more time looking at my cheatsheet than the exam packet itself.

## CS 70 (Spring 25)
The concepts of this course are fascinating. Unfortunately, I feel like this course (or at least, the iteration I took) is too stuffed with content. I found it difficult to digest and properly learn the concepts of the course. To this day, I have no clue what the Chinese Remainder Theorem actually says.

The midterm was absolutely cooked. This was my first experience (and so far, only) with an exam where I genuinely left significant parts blank.

I really enjoyed the probability section. However, I found that the course was too focused on cramming in various formulas related to probability rather than focusing on the big picture concepts. Despite that, CS 70 gave me a wonderful foundation for probability that helped me in EECS 126 and in my research.

## CS 61C (Sum 25)
I took 61C over the summer. I also was a teaching intern for it in Fall 25.

Overall, 61C is a highly useful, very practical course. It felt the most "engineering"-like out of all the CS courses I've taken here. In particular, the content on parallelism was very useful to me (I was able to implement a Joblib-based parallel pipeline in my research using the concepts I learned).

During the summer, I also had an internship at [Pivotal Aero](https://www.pivotal.aero/). Part of my work involved C++. 61C was my first exposure to C and the big ideas of memory management, which was very fortuitous timing.

## CS 188 (Fall 25)
Coming from an Applied Math background, I found the mathematical side of this course to be a bit shallow. The content was useful and interesting -- and the course was quite well-run -- but I wish it went deeper on the math.

I'm not sure I would describe the course as about "Artificial Intelligence." A more apt description would be "61B Part II: Probabilistic Algorithms." The course focused more on teaching equations and algorithms related to the machine learning models (such as HMMs) we were learning about rather than the underlying math of stochastic systems, probability, and estimation.
