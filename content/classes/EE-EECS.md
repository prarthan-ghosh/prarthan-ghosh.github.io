Berkeley has a fantastic EE/EECS department! My interest in EECS, as an Applied Math major, stems from my interest in neuroscience. For this reason, I've found I have little interest in hardware and device physics. Rather, I am absolutely in love with the "Signals & Systems" side of EE/EECS, which is highly relevant to computational neuroscience in particular. Signals, systems, control theory, linear algebra, probability, stochastic systems, machine learning, and other EECS concepts are all highly relevant to computational neuroscience.

## EECS 16A (Fall 24)
I took 16A in my first semester.

16A has been my favorite course at Berkeley by far. In the iteration I took, 16A taught linear algebra without assuming Math 54 as a pre-req. 16A also gave brief exposure to signals concepts -- specifically DTFS, State-Space representation, and BIBO stability.

I particularly enjoyed 16A because I liked its abstract approach. I've never liked number-crunching, and so enjoyed the abstract side of 16A enormously.

In fact, I enjoyed 16A so much that I became a CSM mentor for 16A and also lab staff for the course.

## EECS 16B (Spring 25)
To be honest, I did not enjoy 16B at all. The concepts were interesting enough, but a pure circuits class did not appeal to me at all. I also did not enjoy the fact that most of the course was number-crunching.

The most interesting concepts was transfer functions, filters, and poles & zeros analysis.

## EE 120 (Fall 25)
EE 120 was a fantastic course. As a "sequel" to 16A, it covered a lot of territory that 16A already covered, which was welcome from the point of view of a busy student but less welcome from the point of view of my desire to learn more new concepts.

In particular, I feel like EE 120 could have covered interesting concepts in 2D covolution and transfer function analysis (the latter of which seems to be a big theme of EE 128).

## EECS 126 (Fall 25)
An absolutely fantastic probability course. In my iteration, the midterms were quite difficult, but the final was very easy.

The concepts of this course are very fascinating. In my opinion, probability is one of the most elegant subjects in all of mathematics. Of course, probability is also a very practical subject for anyone interested in AI/ML.

My iteration did not go over information theory, and instead spent its time on hypothesis testing. I found myself more compelled by the probabilistic and stochastic processes side of the course rather than the statistical side.
